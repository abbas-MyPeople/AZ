QR table ordering: pros and cons for full-service restaurants

QR order-and-pay speeds up ordering, ends unpaid tabs and frees staff to run food and look after guests. The costs: fewer guests tip, some guests dislike scanning, and the bigger-ticket lift vendors advertise did not show up in our own numbers. It suits casual sit-down restaurants with repeat orders and groups. It suits fine dining least.

Ask if it fits your room Pros and cons

Written by Abbas Zoeb, founder of AZ Restaurant Partners. Facts checked October 2026.

The short version

Pros and cons at a glance

Cons

  • Tips fall. At our proof restaurant 54% of QR dine-in tickets carried a tip.
  • It changes the service. Staff explain the new routine and guests adjust (Lightspeed lists this as a main con).
  • Fees. Lightspeed cites about $50 to $150+ a month plus 2.8% to 5%+ per transaction for typical providers.
  • Slower payouts when payments run through the provider.
  • Some guests will not scan, so you still need a person on the floor.

Pros

  • No unpaid tabs. The kitchen cooks only what is paid.
  • Faster first order and second round. Nobody waits to flag a server.
  • No keying errors. The guest's order goes straight to the ticket.
  • Menus in the guest's language, with photos and pairings.
  • Fewer people on the floor on slow shifts.

Doing it well

How to run QR ordering without losing the hospitality

Give each table its own code

The code should know the table, so the kitchen and runner know where food goes without asking.

Keep a host on the floor

Greet every table, explain how it works in one sentence, and take an order by hand for anyone who prefers it.

Make the second round one tap

The same code reopens the table's order. Guests add drinks or dessert and pay for just that.

Design the tip screen with care

Show it at payment, keep the choices simple, and test changes against your tip rate.

Offer languages and a talk option

Let guests pick their language first. A voice option helps older guests add dishes.

Print paid tickets with the table number

The kitchen sees the table, the items and that it is paid. Nothing unpaid reaches the line.

Before you sign

Questions to ask any QR ordering vendor

  • Does the ticket print in my kitchen only after payment? If staff must re-enter orders, you keep the keying errors.
  • Does it write to my POS? Orders, items, modifiers and tips should land in the same reports as every other sale.
  • What is the full fee? Monthly fee, per-transaction fee, card processing, and who pays each.
  • When do I get paid? Ask for the payout schedule in writing.
  • Can a table add a second round and split the bill? Test it with four phones at one table.
  • Which languages does the menu support? Ask to see your own menu in them.
  • Who fixes it on a Friday night? Get the support hours and a real escalation path.
Who it fits

Is QR ordering right for your restaurant?

Good fitCasual sit-down, big menus, families and groups, many repeat orders, guests who read other languages, short-staffed nights.
Mixed fitBusy bars (a pre-authorised tab may work better), places with many older regulars (keep a person taking orders by hand).
Poor fitFine dining, wine-led service and tasting menus, where the server is the experience.

Questions owners ask

Is QR code ordering good for full-service restaurants?

For casual sit-down restaurants, usually yes: faster ordering, no unpaid tabs, fewer staff needed. For fine dining, usually no, because the server is part of the experience.

Does QR ordering increase average order value?

Vendors report increases; Lightspeed cites 20% to 60% from Mr Yum's data. At our proof restaurant QR dine-in tickets averaged smaller than staff-taken ones, partly because groups split payment. Measure your own.

Do customers tip less with QR ordering?

In our data, yes: 54% of QR dine-in tickets carried a tip. A clear tip screen and a host who checks in help.

How much does QR table ordering cost?

Lightspeed cites typical fees of about $50 to $150+ a month plus 2.8% to 5%+ per transaction. Your POS may include a version. See our comparison for named vendors.

What about guests who will not scan?

Keep a host who takes orders by hand and a printed menu. QR ordering should be the easy path, not the only one.
