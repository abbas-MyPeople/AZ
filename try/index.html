1 · Get found

Someone asks an AI assistant where to eat tonight.

Most restaurant websites were built in a page builder that blocks crawlers, so assistants quietly answer with the delivery apps. The system publishes a machine-readable menu, hours and facts, measures how often assistants name the restaurant, and runs a weekly engine that proposes pages from real search demand and ships only what the owner approves.

Today

  • The website returns 403 to bots.Ours did. ChatGPT, Gemini and Perplexity could not read the menu or the hours.
  • Google's "Order online" button lists DoorDash first and the restaurant's own page last, if at all.
  • An SEO agency sends a monthly PDF.Nobody reads it, nothing changes, and the invoice is bigger than the ad budget.
  • Nobody knows which searches bring people in.

On the system

  • 7 of 10 AI answers to local food questions name the restaurant
  • 74 pages in the sitemap, one per dish, suburb and occasion
  • Weekly proposals from search data; owner approves by email

One proposal this week. A page for "biryani catering Spring TX".

Evidence: 254 catering searches in the last 90 days with no page that answers them. Catering trays are the highest-margin line. The page would carry real tray prices and the two-hour notice.
  • Owner replies "yes"
  • Page written from the live menu and real terms, checked for invented facts
  • Published, sitemap updated, search engines pinged
  • Measured: orders from that page, not visits

What the engine reads

Google Search data, sales by dish and hour, the weather, the calendar and the live site. It proposes at most a few pages a week and refuses to write a page it cannot ground in a real price or a real term.

What it will not do

Invent a fact, publish a rating as markup, make a near-duplicate page per suburb, or ship without the owner's yes. Visibility that does not turn into orders is retired.

2 · Win the order

A table of six, a phone call in Urdu, and a catering request at 11:46 PM. Same brain.

The guest's own phone becomes the server, and Haniya is the digital server behind it: at the table you hold a button and talk to her; on the phone she answers, knows the whole menu, the hours and the caller, takes the order, texts a pay link and stays on the line while they pay. Catering is booked and paid while the owner sleeps.

Today

  • One server, four tables, the phone in her apron.She answers it at the table, loses the order, or lets it ring.
  • A caller who speaks Urdu gets "sorry, English only" and calls the place that does.
  • Catering is a voicemail that gets heard tomorrow, after the office booked elsewhere.
  • The specials, the sizes and the spice level live in the head of whoever picked up.

On the system

  • 7 languages at the table
  • Almost every dine-in order now placed on the guest's phone
  • Open while closed: after-hours orders are scheduled for opening

3 · Take the money

Nothing reaches the kitchen until it is paid. Then the system chases what is not.

Table, pickup, phone and catering all pay online first. A phone order becomes a pre-built cart link by text. A declined card is explained in plain words with another way to pay, and every decline is logged by cause, which is how the restaurant found out its own fraud rules were refusing more cards than the banks were.

Today

  • Cook first, then ask for the card.Walkouts, "I'll pay when I pick up" that never picks up, cards read out over the phone.
  • A decline is "the machine's acting up".Nobody knows it was the ZIP rule on the terminal.
  • A dispute arrives by mail 40 days later and nobody can find the receipt.

On the system

  • Every app order paid before it cooked, 60 days running
  • 15 of 19 phone pay links paid without a human chasing
  • 6 in 10 app orders pay with Apple Pay or Google Pay

4 · Cook and serve

One ticket stream. Table, phone, website and three delivery apps, in cooking order.

Before: a tablet per delivery app, staff re-keying each order into the register so a ticket would print, and in a rush, orders lost, late or wrong. Now every door lands in one queue with the table number, the guest's own words and a pickup time computed from what the kitchen has in hand.

Today

  • Four tablets beeping on the pass.Accept on the tablet, type it again into the register, hope you typed it right.
  • "No onions" was said to the server and never reached the wok.
  • Every pickup is "20 minutes" whether the kitchen has two tickets or twenty.
  • The printer ran out of paper at 7 PM. Nobody noticed until a guest came to the counter.

On the system

  • 1 in 6 app tickets carries a note the guest typed themselves
  • Honest pickup times from the last 30 minutes of kitchen load
  • A text to the owner if a paid order fails to print

5 · The owner's day

The owner states an outcome in one line. Haniya plans the job, runs it, repeats what repeats, and reports.

This is not a chat box. Every ask becomes a job: a plan built from a registry of what she can do, steps that run in parallel where they can, a gate only where money, food or something destructive is touched, a schedule for the parts that repeat, and a report of what was done, what was assumed and what could not be done. Every step is logged with its cost. When a capability is missing, a step builds it. When she sees something in sales, reviews or calls, she proposes the job herself.

Today

  • "We're out of lemonade" is shouted across the room.The website still sells it. DoorDash still sells it. The phone person forgets.
  • Last night's numbers are a Z-report on a spike and a feeling about whether it was busy.
  • A new dish takes a week: the register, three app portals, a photo from a phone, a post if someone remembers, and nobody texts the people who would want it.
  • Software does what it was built to do.A new kind of ask means a new vendor, a new login, a new invoice.

On the system

  • Outcomes, not tasks: she picks the channels and the steps
  • Plan once, "Do it": outward steps run; money, food and deletes wait for his tap
  • Every step logged with model, latency and cost
  • Missing a capability? A step builds it

6 · Keep the guest, grow the margin

The delivery apps keep the customer. The system knows every guest by name.

Every order through any door adds to one guest record: name, language, usual order, spice, last visit. Texts go out on each guest's own rhythm, never a discount to a regular, with a holdout to measure the truth. Reviews, ads and the documents the brand rests on are watched the same way.

Today

  • The regular is "the butter chicken guy".His number is in DoorDash's database, not the restaurant's.
  • Marketing is 20% off on a Tuesday, given to the people who were coming anyway.
  • Ads run on autopilot and nobody can say which order came from one.
  • The halal certificate on the wall expired last year. It looks the same as a valid one.

On the system

  • Thousands of guests known by name, 9 in 10 reachable
  • Never percent-off, never a discount to a regular
  • Ads judged on net profit per order and switched off by themselves

7 · Under the hood

What runs where, and what this page fakes.

The real stack

PhoneVapi for the call leg, Deepgram multilingual ear, the restaurant's own brain behind a custom-LLM endpoint. 28 tools, every call recorded and replayable.
BrainClaude. One prompt stack: engine rules, an industry playbook, the restaurant's settings, and house rules it learned from the owner.
ServerPython on Fly.io, one machine, one volume, one gunicorn worker with threads. 20 watcher jobs inside it.
TillClover. Menu synced out to every channel; paid orders written in; delivery-app menus repriced from one rule.
TextsTwilio. STOP covers every channel; test and staff numbers can never receive guest texts.
Site and orderingStatic pages on GitHub Pages, one ordering app built four ways (dine-in, takeout, tables, catering) from one source.
EnginesCron: the SEO engine, reviews reader, ads tuner, morning brief, nightly recap, certificate watch. An always-on operator proposes work; the owner approves numbered items.
DeploysA push requests a deploy; it waits until no call and no checkout is live, then ships. Never over a live call.

What this page fakes

  • Everything. The page's security policy is default-src 'none'. No fetch, no fonts, no images from anywhere. Open the Network tab.
  • The restaurant. Mehfil Grill does not exist. Menu, prices, guests and numbers inside the widgets are sample data.
  • The brain. Haniya's answers here are scripted. On the real line she reasons over the live menu, the caller's history and the kitchen's load.
  • Voice. Off, because it costs money per minute. The real table and the real phone both take speech.

What is measured, not faked

  • The numbers in the scoreboard at the top and the "On the system" facts are from the real restaurant, re-checked in October 2026.
  • The decline breakdown, the pay-link ladder, the call situations and the owner commands are real events, replayed on the sample menu.
