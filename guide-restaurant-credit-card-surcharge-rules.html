Restaurant credit card surcharges: the rules, the Texas problem and the safer options

Visa lets a US merchant surcharge credit cards only, capped at its real cost of acceptance and 3%, with 30 days notice and a separate line on the receipt (Visa, 2023 version, checked Nov 2026).

Texas law still says a seller may not add a credit card surcharge, and sources disagree on whether that can be enforced today (Texas OCCC bulletin, checked Nov 2026). A Texas restaurant should use one of the state's five listed alternatives, or get a written answer from a Texas lawyer first.

Texas

Why Texas is the hard one

Texas Finance Code Section 339.001(a) says "a seller may not impose a surcharge on a buyer who uses a credit card for an extension of credit instead of cash, a check, or a similar means of payment" (Texas OCCC bulletin, checked Nov 2026). A federal appeals court upheld the law against a free-speech challenge in 2016 (Rowell v. Pettijohn, 5th Cir., Mar 2, 2016, checked Nov 2026).

After that the picture gets unclear. A payments vendor says the law was later held unconstitutional as applied and the state agreed not to enforce it (Lone Star Payment, Jul 2026, checked Nov 2026). Another says courts struck down or enjoined enforcement years ago (Katalyst, checked Nov 2026). A third guide says the state attorney general still treats the law as enforceable (Merchant Cost Consulting, search copy, checked Nov 2026). We could not confirm any of those from a court order or a current state source. Until a Texas lawyer tells you otherwise in writing, treat a line-item credit card surcharge in Texas as not allowed.

The state regulator, the Office of Consumer Credit Commissioner (OCCC), has published five options that are not prohibited surcharges. The bulletin dates from 2015, so confirm it still applies.

OptionHow it worksWatch out for
Same price for everyoneOne price, however the guest pays.Your card cost has to live inside the menu price.
Uniform convenience feeOne fee for all payment methods, disclosed before the sale. The state attorney general's Opinion GA-0951 (2012) approved a standard service fee on all transactions.It must be truly uniform. A fee that makes card payers pay more than others is a surcharge.
Cash discountThe regular price is the card price, and cash, check or debit pays less.You cannot combine this with a convenience fee.
A no-fee card optionOffer a card method with no fee, and a second card method with one.At least one card path has to be free.
Third-party processor feeA processor charges the guest a higher card fee, with no money or benefit going to you.You must have an arm's-length, payments-only relationship, and guests must have another way to pay.

The math

What a surcharge recovers

On a $40 ticket, a 3% surcharge is $1.20. On SpotOn's published rates (checked Nov 2026), that same $40 card sale costs $1.13 on its 2.45% + $0.15 plan and $1.32 on its 2.79% + $0.20 plan. Under Visa's rule you cannot charge more than your cost, so you need your real rate before you pick a number. Our effective processing rate page shows how to find it, and what a POS really costs shows how card fees compare with software.

What to do

Five steps, in order

  1. Find your real card cost.Divide last month's total card fees by card sales. Do that before anything else.
  2. Compare with a small price rise.For many rooms, a modest menu increase is simpler. See how to raise prices without losing guests.
  3. Check your state.Confirm the table above with your processor or lawyer. In Texas, pick from the five options or get a written answer from a Texas lawyer first.
  4. Ask your processor in writing.Can your POS tell debit from credit, print the fee on its own receipt line, and has the 30-day notice been filed? Then check what your POS supports.
  5. Tell guests before they order.One plain line on the menu, the door and the checkout screen. Guests should never find a fee for the first time on the receipt.

Questions owners ask

Can a restaurant charge a credit card fee?

In most US states, yes, within card network rules: credit only, no more than your cost and 3% on Visa, 30 days notice, and a separate receipt line. Some states ban or limit it, so check yours first.

Is a credit card surcharge legal in Texas?

Texas Finance Code 339.001 still says a seller may not add one. A federal appeals court upheld it in 2016. Vendors now disagree on whether it can be enforced, so treat it as not allowed unless a Texas lawyer says otherwise in writing.

Can a Texas restaurant offer a cash discount?

Yes. The Texas consumer credit regulator says the law does not prohibit discounts for paying by cash, check or debit. The regular price must be the card price, and you cannot combine it with a convenience fee.

Can you surcharge debit cards?

No, not under Visa's rules. Visa says debit and prepaid cannot be surcharged, so your POS needs to tell debit from credit.

Can a restaurant add a service fee in Texas?

A uniform fee on all payment methods, disclosed before the sale, is not a prohibited credit card surcharge under the state regulator's bulletin. It must not make card payers pay more than anyone else.
