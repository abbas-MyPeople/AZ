How to control AI menu photos on DoorDash and Uber Eats

Delivery apps can now retouch, match or add menu photos, sometimes with AI. The best protection is a real photo of every item in your own account, because uploading your own picture replaces a DoorDash-added one (DoorDash, checked Nov 2026).

Check every app once a month, and ask each app in writing to stop adding photos if you do not want them.

Why it matters

What happened, and where apps stand

Just Eat apologized in October after an AI test showed bacon and eggs in a fizzy drink for a Belfast cafe's soda bread, and turned a "cheese roll" into sushi. It said it was removing the wrong images and would stop making more until the AI improved (BBC via AOL, Oct 21, 2026, checked Nov 2026). That was in the UK. US apps sell similar tools.

The steps

Eight steps to keep your photos yours

  1. Look at your menu the way a guest does.Open each app on a phone, logged out of your merchant account, and go through every item. Screenshot anything wrong.
  2. Make a list of what is missing or wrong.Empty photo slots are what apps fill in, so missing pictures come first. Sort by your top sellers.
  3. Shoot real photos of your top items.Daylight, a clean table, one plate. Uber Eats says "every restaurant partner can get a free professional photo shoot of their menu" (Uber Eats, checked Nov 2026), so ask for it.
  4. Upload to DoorDash.In the Merchant Portal go to Menu Manager and add your photo to the item. A photo you upload replaces one DoorDash added (DoorDash, checked Nov 2026).
  5. Use AI Retouch only on purpose.If you try it, compare the result to the plate you serve. Keep the dish exactly as it is, and click reset if it drifts (DoorDash help, checked Nov 2026).
  6. Ask DoorDash to stop adding photos.DoorDash says to contact Merchant Support if you do not want it adding photos. For Yelp-sourced photos, use Request Menu Help in Menu Manager and include the category, item name, price and menu (DoorDash, checked Nov 2026).
  7. Upload to Uber Eats.In Uber Eats Manager open Menu Maker, go to the Items tab, click the item and use the Photo section. Click Save to submit it. The item stays locked until the photo is approved (Uber Eats, checked Nov 2026).
  8. Ask Uber Eats about the rest, in writing.Uber's Australia and New Zealand post says owners can remove customer photos in Menu Maker and reach support through the chat icon in Uber Eats Manager (Uber, checked Nov 2026). Ask whether the same applies to you, and whether AI enhancement can be switched off for your store.
The rule to follow

What your photo has to show

DoorDash says each photo must show what a customer receives, and rejects photos that do not match the item (DoorDash help, checked Nov 2026). Its virtual brand rules bar "evidently AI-generated images, heavily altered images" and name distorted food shapes and inaccurate ingredients as problems (DoorDash help, checked Nov 2026). That page covers virtual brands, so treat it as a guide for any listing.

  • Same dish, same portion, same plate your kitchen sends.
  • Nothing on the plate that the guest will not receive.
  • Update the photo when the dish or the portion changes.
  • Keep a dated folder of your approved photos, so a wrong picture is easy to replace.

Questions owners ask

Can DoorDash add photos to my menu?

Yes. DoorDash says it can match your Instagram photos to items with no picture, and some photos come from Yelp. It says it will never replace photos you already have.

How do I remove an AI photo from DoorDash?

Upload your own photo for that item in Menu Manager. To stop DoorDash adding photos, contact Merchant Support. For Yelp photos use Request Menu Help and include the category, item name, price and menu.

Does Uber Eats use AI on food photos?

Yes. Uber says it uses AI to detect and enhance low-quality food images, improving lighting, resolution, framing and plating. We found no public way to switch this off, so ask Uber support.

Are AI food photos allowed on delivery apps?

DoorDash offers its own AI retouching, and says each photo must show what the customer receives. Its virtual brand rules bar evidently AI-generated images. Other apps vary, so check before you upload.

What should a menu photo show?

The dish as your kitchen sends it: same portion, same plate, nothing extra. Upload real photos of your top sellers first, since empty slots are what apps fill.

Does Uber Eats do free menu photos?

Uber Eats says every restaurant partner can get a free professional photo shoot of their menu. Ask through Uber Eats Manager.
