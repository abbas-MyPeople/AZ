ezCater alternatives for restaurants: fees and options compared

ezCater charges restaurants a 15% commission on marketplace orders, 7% on orders through its online ordering on your own website, and a 2.99% payment fee, with no signup or monthly fee (ezCater help centre, checked Oct 2026). Uber agreed on October 6, 2026 to buy ezCater for $2.3 billion, and no fee change has been announced.

The alternatives split in two. Marketplaces like Grubhub, DoorDash, CaterCow and ZeroCater find you new office buyers, and none of them publishes its restaurant fee. Direct catering ordering on your own POS costs a software fee and card processing, and every repeat order stays with you. Most independents should use one marketplace to get found and move repeat buyers direct.

What ezCater asks of you

ezCater's rules worth knowing

  • Equal price guarantee. ezCater buyers must never pay more than buyers ordering from you directly (ezCater onboarding requirements, checked Oct 2026). You cannot add the commission to your ezCater menu.
  • Entry bar. A physical restaurant, six months or more of online reviews, and 3.5 stars or higher on Yelp or Google.
  • Do the math on one order. On a $400 marketplace order, the 15% commission is $60 and the 2.99% payment fee is about $12 on the food alone, more once tax and delivery are added. That is roughly $72 before your food cost.
  • The 7% rate applies when buyers order through ezCater's online ordering on your website, so moving repeat buyers there cuts the fee by more than half.
The Uber deal

What changes when Uber owns ezCater

Uber agreed to buy ezCater for $2.3 billion in cash. The deal still needs regulatory approval (Nation's Restaurant News, Oct 6, 2026, checked Oct 2026). Uber says it will bring ezCater into Uber Eats and Uber for Business. Nothing has been said about restaurant fees. We cover the deal itself in what the Uber and ezCater deal means for your catering.

So what: if most of your catering runs through one marketplace, you now depend on whatever Uber decides. Spreading across two channels, and owning the repeat buyers, protects you either way.

Who should use which

Our recommendation by situation

Use a marketplace when

  • Offices near you do not know you yet.
  • You can deliver on time, or the marketplace delivers for you.
  • You will follow up with every first-time buyer so the next order comes direct.

Go direct when

  • The buyer has ordered before.
  • Orders are large enough that 15% hurts.
  • You want deposits or full payment before cooking.

Questions owners ask

How much does ezCater charge restaurants?

ezCater's help centre lists a 15% commission on ezCater Marketplace orders, 7% on orders through its online ordering on your own website, and a 2.99% payment transaction fee on the meal subtotal, delivery fee and tax. There are no signup or monthly fees.

What are the best ezCater alternatives for restaurants?

For new corporate buyers: DoorDash Corporate Catering, Grubhub catering, Uber group orders, CaterCow, ZeroCater, Fooda and Forkable, depending on your city. For repeat buyers: catering ordering on your own website and POS, which has no marketplace commission.

Is Uber buying ezCater?

Yes. Uber agreed on October 6, 2026 to buy ezCater for $2.3 billion in cash. The deal needs regulatory approval. No change to restaurant fees had been announced as of October 2026.

Can I charge more on ezCater to cover the fee?

No. ezCater's onboarding rules include an equal price guarantee: ezCater buyers must not pay more than people who order from you directly.

How do I get catering orders without a marketplace?

Build a list of offices, churches, schools and teams within a few miles, offer a simple tray menu with clear headcounts, take payment online before you cook, and follow up after every order so the next one comes straight to you.
