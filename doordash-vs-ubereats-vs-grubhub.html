DoorDash vs Uber Eats vs Grubhub: what the delivery apps really cost

DoorDash lists 15%, 25% or 30% on delivery and 6% on pickup. Uber Eats lists 20%, 25% or 30%, with some cities starting at 15%, and 7% on pickup. Grubhub's own page lists a 5%, 15% or 20% marketing commission, delivery fees that "start at 10%" and processing on top, so get its total in writing. Checked Nov 2026.

For independent & family-run restaurants · ~7 min read

The short version

DoorDash, Uber Eats and Grubhub are more alike than different. Each one sells you a menu of commission tiers where a lower percentage buys less visibility, each layers on optional advertising, and each keeps the customer relationship for itself. The "best" app is not a fixed answer - it's whichever one actually has hungry customers in your delivery radius, on the tier you'd realistically run. Below is how they line up on the things that hit your bank account.

Two things jump out. First, the headline commission is only part of the bill - pickup rates, ad spend and promo co-pays all stack on top. Second, on the thing that matters most long-term, all three are identical: they keep the customer. That single fact shapes everything below.

How the three actually differ

DoorDash has the largest US market share in most suburban markets, which usually means the most order volume - and its Storefront product gives you commission-free ordering on your own website, a genuinely useful lever if you lean into it. Uber Eats benefits from the Uber rides app funneling in members and tends to be strongest in dense, urban delivery zones. Grubhub uniquely breaks its cut into a separate "marketing" commission and a "delivery" commission, and remains strongest across parts of the Northeast. None of that changes the core economics; it changes which app is likely to bring you the most orders where you actually are.

DoorDash vs Grubhub for merchants: the main differences

Reach. Consumer Edge data put DoorDash at 59% of spending across major delivery apps in Q3 2026, against 18% for Uber Eats. Grubhub was not in the figures we read. The data covers all delivery categories, not only restaurants (NRN, Oct 29, 2026). Plans. DoorDash publishes three plans with a flat 6% pickup rate. Grubhub's reported Basic tier relies on your own drivers, and its network tiers cost more. Payouts. DoorDash pays weekly, with daily deposits available through Fast Pay (DoorDash). We could not confirm Grubhub's schedule from Grubhub itself, so check your account. Trials. DoorDash and Uber Eats both advertise intro offers on higher plans. Read the end date before you switch.

Uber Eats vs Grubhub: who pays more?

The restaurant pays about the same on plans that use the app's drivers. On a $40 delivery order, Uber Eats at 25% takes $10. Grubhub at about 20-25% takes about $8 to $10, plus about $1.52 in processing on Sauce's figures. Grubhub's own page lists marketing rates of 15% or 20% and delivery fees from 10%, so ask it for the all-in rate. Uber Eats does not list a separate processing fee on the pricing page we read, so ask. The gap is small enough that order volume in your area matters more. This covers what restaurants pay. Driver pay is a separate question we do not cover.

Which delivery app has the best advertising options?

All three sell paid placement on top of commission. Uber Eats publishes the clearest deal: any plan can buy ads, and Premium matches ad spend with up to $100 a month in credit (Uber Eats). Grubhub says its plans earn a monthly ad credit equal to your commission rate (Grubhub, checked Nov 2026). Sauce says Grubhub charges sponsored placement as a per-order fee on top of commission (Sauce, Jan 2026). DoorDash has the largest share of delivery spending, so its ads have the widest audience. Test one app for 30 days with a set budget and count new guests, since repeat guests would have ordered anyway. Our ad tracking guide shows how.

Which is right for you

Don't pick on the lowest advertised percentage. Pick on three questions:

Where are your customers? Whichever app is dominant in your ZIP code will usually out-earn a cheaper tier on a weaker app. Ask nearby operators, or run a short test on each before committing ad dollars.

What tier will you really run? The base ~15% tier buries you in search results. Most restaurants that want volume end up on a middle or top tier nearer 25-30%, so budget for that reality, not the floor price.

How much can you self-fulfil? If you have your own driver or a tight radius, the lower pickup and self-delivery rates - plus DoorDash Storefront-style first-party ordering - can dramatically beat full marketplace commission. That's where the margin actually lives.

For most independents the answer isn't one app - it's running the one or two strongest apps in your area at a tier you can afford, while steadily building first-party ordering so you're not renting your whole customer base at 30%.

The honest take: DoorDash, Uber Eats and Grubhub are close enough on price that the deciding factor is local demand, not the commission table. Use the apps for discovery, run them on the tier that actually gets you seen, price your delivery menu to absorb the cut, and put real effort into converting those orders into direct regulars. That combination beats chasing the "cheapest app" every time. We take no commission from any platform - this comparison is for reference only.

Frequently asked questions

How does Grubhub charge merchants?

Grubhub's own page lists a 5% Basic, 15% Plus or 20% All-access marketing commission, delivery fees that start at 10%, and processing on top, and describes ad credits equal to your commission rate. Ask for one all-in rate in writing.

What are the main differences between DoorDash and Grubhub for merchants?

DoorDash has far more delivery spending, three published plans at 15%, 25% and 30%, and 6% pickup. Grubhub's own page lists a 5%, 15% or 20% marketing commission and delivery fees from 10%, with processing separate; Sauce reports about 15% to 30% in total. Local demand should decide.

How do DoorDash and Grubhub compare on merchant payouts?

DoorDash pays weekly by default and offers daily deposits through Fast Pay. We could not confirm Grubhub's schedule from Grubhub itself, so check your account. Both deduct commissions and fees before you are paid, so read the statement behind each deposit.

How does DoorDash compare with Uber Eats for restaurants?

Rates are close: DoorDash 15%, 25%, 30% with 6% pickup, and Uber Eats 20%, 25%, 30% with 7% pickup when in-store pricing is validated. DoorDash has the larger share of spending, at 59% vs 18% in Q3 2026 across all delivery categories. Uber Eats publishes a 15% self-delivery rate.

Which delivery apps offer the best advertising options for restaurants?

Uber Eats publishes the clearest offer: ads on any plan, and up to $100 a month in matched credit on Premium. DoorDash reaches the largest audience. Grubhub's plans earn an ad credit equal to your commission rate. Test one app for 30 days and count only new guests.

Uber Eats vs Grubhub: who pays more?

For restaurants, about the same on plans using the app's drivers. A $40 order at Uber Eats' 25% costs $10. Grubhub at about 20-25% costs about $8 to $10, plus about $1.52 in processing. Check the fee lines in your own statement.

Which delivery app charges the lowest commission?

There is no single winner. DoorDash lists 15%, 25% and 30%, Uber Eats lists 20%, 25% and 30% with some cities starting at 15%, and Grubhub lists 5%, 15% or 20% marketing commission with delivery fees from 10%, reported at about 15% to 30% in total. The lowest rate usually buys the least visibility, so compare the plan you would really use.

Do I have to be on all three apps?

No. Many independents start on the one or two apps strongest in their area, then add a third only if the demand justifies it. More apps widens discovery but multiplies tablets, menu upkeep and fees. Middleware makes multiple apps manageable, but more apps is not automatically more profit.

Is the commission the only fee?

No. On top of the delivery commission you'll typically see pickup/self-delivery rates, optional sponsored-listing ad spend, promotion co-pays, and error or refund adjustments deducted from payouts. The all-in cost is usually higher than the headline percentage alone.

How often do the delivery apps pay out?

DoorDash pays weekly by default, with daily deposits through Fast Pay. Uber Eats and Grubhub do not state a schedule on the pages we could read, so confirm it and any fast-pay charge in your own dashboard.
