## Reality bible (every shot must pass)
- **Physics.** Everything has weight. A phone on a wall vibrates against its mount and the cord swings; a ticket curls as it prints; a wok toss has wind-up, lift, catch and the food arcs under gravity; bags sag with what's inside; a coin lands, bounces once and settles. Shadows touch the floor; nothing floats unless it's a screen card, and screen cards rise from their device on a beam of light.
- **Logic.** Cause comes before effect, on screen. The ticket prints only after the pay link is paid, because that's how it works. Every screen shows only what that person would really see, worded exactly as our real screens word it. No step happens that our system doesn't do.
- **Time.** The HUD clock is the truth. Time compresses only in the open: the clock spins forward with a whoosh (7:16 to 7:31) while the food cooks. The food never appears ready in two seconds.
- **Continuity.** Every object stays where it was left. The same chef, the same apron, the same plate count, the same moon. The street lights are on the whole time; the restaurant's warm light spills onto the pavement in every exterior shot.
- **Camera.** Real lenses (35mm for the room, 50 to 85mm for faces and hands), a real camera body: it eases in and out, never snaps, and never passes through a wall except through the cut-away front we establish in shot 1. Motion blur on everything moving, shallow focus on close-ups.
- **Characters.** Real proportions, real hands with five fingers, faces that act: the caller's relief, the chef's calm, the owner's shoulders dropping. People shift their weight, breathe, and look at what they touch.
- **The weight lifts.** The owner carries the night as a visible load: small icons of what needs them (a ringing phone, a waiting order, a bill) gathered over their shoulders. Each time the system takes one, it lifts off and the owner straightens. By the end the owner is free to do the one thing only they can: greet a guest.
- **Easy to explain.** One idea per line, one card per idea, three elements per card at most, readable at phone size with the sound off. If a frame needs a second look to understand, it gets redrawn.
- **Entertaining.** A small human story runs underneath: the hungry caller's kids hanging off his arm, cheering when the text lands; the chef who never once looks up. A smile every 10 seconds, never a joke at the guest's expense.
- **Sound.** Every visible action has its sound: the ring, the vibration, the wok, the printer, the text ping, the door. Music sits under your voice and lifts on the close.
