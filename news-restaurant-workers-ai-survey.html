How restaurant workers feel about AI, and what it means for your team

A Toast survey of more than 700 restaurant workers, reported on October 22, 2026, found that half are very or extremely open to using AI at work, while about one in five is not very or not at all open (Nation's Restaurant News, Oct 22, 2026, checked Oct 2026).

Workers want AI for busywork: 54% are very or extremely open to end-of-shift summaries and automated scheduling. For an independent owner, the lesson is to start AI on the chores your staff dislike, and leave the moments they enjoy with guests to them.

What staff want

Where workers want AI, and where they do not

Yes: end-of-shift summaries

54% of workers are very or extremely open to AI writing the shift wrap-up and building the schedule. Those are the most-wanted uses in the survey.

Yes, a little less: upsell tips and training

Upsell suggestions, personalization tips and training drew similar but slightly lower interest. The article gives no exact figures for them.

Protect: time with guests

27% of workers named connecting with customers as a top-three favourite part of the job. AI that takes that away will meet resistance.

What makes them leave

31% say poor hourly pay is the biggest reason they would quit, and 26% say a difficult manager. AI does not fix either, but it can take tasks off a stretched manager.

All figures from NRN, Oct 22, 2026, checked Oct 2026.

So what for one restaurant

What an independent owner should do with this

  1. Ask your team what they hate doing.Closing counts, the schedule, the shift note, answering the same phone question all night. Start AI there.
  2. Say out loud what AI will not do.If the plan is not to cut anyone's hours, tell them. Fear of replacement is the main reason staff quietly work around a new tool.
  3. Keep guest moments human.Greeting, checking on a table, handling a complaint. Use AI to free time for those.
  4. Run it alongside people first.Let the tool and the staff do the job together for a few weeks, and compare.
  5. Show the team the result.Fewer missed calls, less time on the schedule, fewer wrong orders. People adopt tools that visibly help them.

Questions owners ask

Do restaurant workers want to use AI?

Half of the 700-plus workers in Toast's survey, reported October 22, 2026, are very or extremely open to AI at work. Another 27% are somewhat open, and about 22% are not very or not at all open.

What do restaurant staff want AI to do?

End-of-shift summaries and automated scheduling drew the most interest, with 54% very or extremely open. Upsell suggestions, personalization tips and training drew slightly lower interest.

Are restaurant owners more enthusiastic about AI than staff?

Yes, by Toast's data. Separate Toast figures from September found 9 in 10 operators use AI and 87% are comfortable with it, a higher level than workers report.

Why do AI projects fail in restaurants?

Restaurant Dive points to public-facing failures such as drive-thru bots interrupting guests, tools that miscount inventory, extra work for staff who must fix errors, and staff fear of being replaced.

How do I get my staff to accept AI?

Start with chores they dislike, tell them plainly what AI will not do, run it alongside people first and show the team the results. Keep greeting and caring for guests with people.
