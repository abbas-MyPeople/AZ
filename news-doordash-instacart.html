DoorDash is buying Instacart: what it means for independent restaurants

On October 29, 2026, DoorDash agreed to buy Instacart for about $17 billion. The deal may not close until late 2027 or the first half of 2028, and it still needs regulators and Instacart shareholders (Nation's Restaurant News, Oct 29, 2026, checked Nov 2026). We found no announced change to restaurant fees.

Nothing changes on your DoorDash tablet this week. The lesson for an independent is about dependence: one company now takes most delivery spending in the US, so use the next year to move repeat guests onto your own ordering.

What it does not change

Three things that stay the same today

Your commission

DoorDash lists 15%, 25% or 30% on delivery by plan and 6% on pickup, with no monthly fee or cancellation penalty (DoorDash). Check your own plan.

Your competitors

Uber Eats lists 20%, 25% or 30%, with some cities starting Lite at 15%, and pickup at 7% with validated in-store pricing (Uber Eats). Uber remains the second option for your guests.

Your guest list

Guests who order on an app remain mostly the app's guests. Olo's app and your own site take a different approach to who pays and who keeps the contact.

Questions owners ask

Is DoorDash buying Instacart?

Yes, it has agreed to. The deal is worth about $17 billion, or $65 a share, and was announced October 29, 2026. It needs shareholder and regulator approval and may not close until late 2027 or the first half of 2028.

Will DoorDash raise restaurant commissions after buying Instacart?

Nothing has been announced. The coverage we read names no restaurant fee change. DoorDash lists 15%, 25% and 30% delivery plans and 6% pickup today. Watch your merchant notices.

Does the DoorDash Instacart deal affect restaurants?

Not directly today. Instacart is a grocery service. The effect on restaurants is indirect: a larger company, a merged membership and a denser driver network if the deal closes.

What is DoorDash's market share?

Consumer Edge data reported by NRN put DoorDash at 59% of spending across major delivery apps in Q3 2026, with Uber Eats and Instacart at 18% each. It covers all delivery categories, not only restaurants.

Should I leave DoorDash because of this?

No need. Apps bring new guests. Keep them for discovery, price your app menu to cover the fee and move repeat guests to your own ordering. Do restaurants lose money on DoorDash? shows how to check.

Will DashPass and Instacart+ merge?

That is the plan once the deal closes, according to The Next Web, with both services sold under one subscription fee. DoorDash says it will keep the Instacart brand and app.
