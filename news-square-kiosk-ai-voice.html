Square Kiosk with voice AI: what it means for independent restaurants

On October 14, 2026 Square launched a new Square Kiosk: a 21.5-inch standalone touchscreen that can take orders by voice or touch, priced at $1,999 with a countertop stand plus $99 a month in software per device (Fast Casual, Oct 14, 2026, checked Oct 2026).

It requires a Square Plus or Premium plan (Square help center), so one kiosk costs about $3,775 in the first year before card fees, by our math. It suits counter-service lines. It does not help a sit-down room or an unanswered phone.

What is not known yet

Four things to find out before you buy

Real-world accuracy

Square has not published how often the voice layer gets an order right, or how often a guest falls back to touch. The only operator quoted in the coverage is Boba Nation Grand, a chain, which says its kiosks "have been tested successfully."

Languages

Neither Square's help article nor the launch coverage names the languages the voice layer understands. A room where guests order in Urdu, Hindi or Spanish needs that answer in writing.

Noise

Square describes three directional microphones with noise filtering. A busy counter at lunch is the test that matters, so ask for a trial or a return window.

Who it is built for

The launch is framed around quick-service and franchise tools such as shared menus and multi-location reporting. A one-room, full-service restaurant uses very little of that.

So what for one restaurant

Who a voice kiosk fits, and who it does not

A counter kiosk helps when guests stand in a line, order once, pay and wait: a taqueria, a bubble tea shop, a bakery with a counter queue. It takes the order without a cashier and offers an add-on at the right moment. It does nothing for guests who sit down, and nothing for the phone ringing behind the counter.

Many independents want those two other jobs more. If the guest sits at a table, they can scan a code, order and pay on their own phone with no hardware bought. If the guest calls, an AI phone host answers when the line is busy or the kitchen is closed. We compare all three side by side in our kiosk vs QR table ordering vs AI phone host guide. If you already run Square, our Square vs Toast and Square vs Clover pages cover the larger platform choice.

One caution from the whole voice-ordering field. Burger King rethought its drive-thru AI plan (Nation's Restaurant News, Sep 24, 2026), and our AI ordering guide covers why forcing guests to talk to a bot loses them. Always leave touch as the easy path and keep a person within reach.

Before you buy

Six questions to ask your Square rep

  1. Which languages does voice ordering understand today?Get the list in writing, and ask what happens when a guest switches language mid-order.
  2. Can I try one kiosk and return it?Ask for the return window and who pays the shipping and the unused software month.
  3. Which plan do I need, and what does it cost per location?The kiosk needs Plus or Premium. Confirm the price on your own account, not a blog.
  4. How do menu changes reach the kiosk?If you 86 an item on the line, the kiosk should stop selling it in the same minute.
  5. What does voice do when the internet drops?Square says offline and cash payments are not supported. Ask what the guest sees.
  6. How do I see what voice orders went wrong?You need a report of abandoned or corrected orders, otherwise you cannot judge the tool.

Questions owners ask

How much does the new Square Kiosk cost?

Square Kiosk costs $1,999 including a countertop stand, plus $99 a month in software per device, according to Fast Casual on October 14, 2026. It also needs a Square Plus or Premium subscription. Mounting hardware and card processing fees are extra.

Does the Square Kiosk take voice orders?

Yes. It is Square's first point-of-sale device with voice ordering. Guests can speak or tap. Voice is optional, it is off on the idle screen, and the device has no camera.

Does Square Kiosk work offline or take cash?

No. Square's help center says offline and cash payments are not supported, and the kiosk needs stable internet for payments and voice. It is for indoor use only.

Which languages does the Square Kiosk voice ordering support?

Square has not said in its help article or in launch coverage. Ask your Square rep for the list in writing before you buy, especially if guests order in Urdu, Hindi or Spanish.

Is a kiosk better than QR table ordering?

They do different jobs. A kiosk fits a counter line. QR ordering fits guests who sit down, and it needs no hardware. For calls, an AI phone host is the matching tool. Our comparison page lays the three out side by side.
