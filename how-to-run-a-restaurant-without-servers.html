How to run a restaurant without servers, or with far fewer

Move the two jobs that eat a server's shift, taking the order and taking the money, onto the guest's own phone with a QR code at each table, and keep your people for greeting, running food and fixing problems. Most casual full-service restaurants do best with this hybrid. Going fully server-free usually means switching to counter service, which changes how guests tip and how your staff are paid.

Why owners are doing this

The hiring math behind it

The US Bureau of Labor Statistics puts median pay for waiters and waitresses at $16.94 an hour (May 2025) and expects about 423,100 openings every year through 2035, mostly to replace people who leave the job. That churn is the real cost: every new server needs training on the menu, the POS and your regulars.

Under federal law a tipped worker can be paid a cash wage as low as $2.13 an hour, with tips making up the rest to $7.25 (the tip credit). Texas follows the federal numbers. If you move to counter service and tips fall, that credit stops covering the gap and your wage bill per hour can go up even as headcount goes down. Run the numbers for your own staff before you cut anyone.

Step by step

How to make the switch without losing guests

List every job a server does today

Greet, seat, explain the menu, take the order, type it into the POS, run food, refill drinks, check in, print the check, take payment, split the bill, clear the table. Mark which ones a guest could do on a phone.

Move ordering, paying and splitting to the phone

Put a QR code on every table that already knows the table number. The guest orders, pays, splits and tips. The kitchen gets a ticket that says which table and that it is paid.

Keep a person for the human parts

One host greets and explains how the table works. Runners bring food. Anyone on the floor can fix a wrong dish or help someone who does not want to scan.

Let the menu do the server's talking

Photos, short descriptions, spice levels and a suggested side on each dish. Offer the menu in the languages your guests read. Ours runs in English plus Urdu, Hindi, Gujarati, Punjabi, Arabic and Spanish.

Make a second round easy

The same code should open the same table's order so guests can add drinks or dessert and pay for just that.

Settle tips and pay before you launch

Decide how tips are shared and whether base pay changes. Remember federal rules: tips belong to the employee, and an employer may not keep any part of them.

Watch three numbers for a month

Share of orders the guest places, unpaid or voided tickets, and tip rate. If tips sink, change the tip screen or the host's script before changing the model.

Be honest with yourself

When fewer servers is the wrong call

Where it hurts

  • Tips drop. Guests tip less on a phone screen than to a person. Plan how your team gets paid before launch.
  • Bigger tickets are not guaranteed. Vendors publish order-size lifts. In our own 60 days, QR dine-in tickets averaged smaller than staff-taken ones (some tables split across phones).
  • Fine dining, wine lists and tasting menus need a person explaining and pacing the meal.
  • Some guests will not scan. You need a human path for them every service.

Where it works

  • Casual sit-down with a big menu that guests reorder from.
  • Groups and families who want to add a round without waiting.
  • Short-staffed nights, where one host and a runner cover the room.
  • Guests who read the menu more comfortably in their own language.

Questions owners ask

Can a sit-down restaurant really run without servers?

Yes, if guests order and pay on their phone and you keep a host and food runners. Fully server-free usually means counter service. Most casual sit-down restaurants land in between.

Do guests tip when they order on a QR code?

Fewer do. At our proof restaurant 54% of QR dine-in tickets carried a tip after we moved to pay-first. Plan staff pay with that in mind.

What is the cheapest way to start?

Use the QR ordering your POS already offers (Toast, Square and Clover all have a version) on a few tables during your slowest shift, and measure tickets, tips and complaints for two weeks.

Will older guests be left out?

Only if nobody is on the floor. Keep a host who can take an order by hand, and give guests a large-text, language-first menu. Ours also lets a guest talk to add dishes.
