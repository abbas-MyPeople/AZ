FTC sues Toast: what it means for independent restaurants

On October 29, 2026, the Federal Trade Commission and eight states sued Toast, the restaurant POS company, alleging hidden fees, add-ons owners never agreed to and contracts that are hard to cancel (FTC, Oct 29, 2026, checked Nov 2026). Toast disputes the claims and says it will fight in court. No court has ruled.

If you are on Toast, you do not need to do anything drastic this week. Read your own contract, line up your last three statements against it, and note your renewal date. If you are shopping for a POS, get the same items in writing from every vendor.

Toast's answer

What Toast says back

  • It "strongly" disagrees and is "prepared to defend our business and fight this unprecedented action through litigation" (NRN).
  • Its restaurant customers negotiate their contracts, and it says the FTC "disregarded the information we provided" on that (Restaurant Dive).
  • No court has applied ROSCA to negotiated business-to-business contracts, and Toast does not believe the law covers them (Restaurant Dive).
  • "Nothing about today's news changes our customer service, pricing, or support" (NRN).

Expect this to take a while. A lawsuit does not change your contract today, and nothing in the coverage we read offers refunds to owners yet.

So what for one restaurant

What to do, depending on where you stand

You are on Toast and happy

Stay. Pull your agreement, note the end date and the notice period, and check each add-on line. Fifteen minutes now avoids an auto-renewal surprise later.

You are on Toast and unhappy

Do not cancel in anger. Work out the exit cost first, then time your notice. Our guide to cancelling a POS contract walks through it in order.

You are shopping for a POS

Ask each vendor for the same list in writing: card rate, contract length, renewal terms, termination formula, add-on prices. See Toast alternatives compared.

You are on another POS

Questions owners ask

Why is the FTC suing Toast?

The FTC and eight states allege Toast used hidden fees, charged for add-ons owners did not agree to, made false claims about $0 plans and 24/7 support, and made cancelling hard. Toast disputes this and no court has ruled.

Is the Toast lawsuit a class action?

No. It is a government enforcement case filed by the FTC and state officials, not a class action brought by private owners. It asks for a court order, monetary damages and civil penalties.

What is Toast's early termination fee?

The FTC alleges an undisclosed fee of $150 for each month left on a contract, and cites one owner charged $5,318.87. Press reports do not say whether it still applies, so read the termination clause in your own agreement.

Will Toast owners get refunds?

Not yet. The suit asks the court for monetary damages, but no court has ruled and no refund process has been announced in the coverage we read.

Should I leave Toast because of the lawsuit?

The lawsuit alone is not a reason to switch. Decide on your own bill: compare your total monthly cost and exit fee with a written quote from another POS. Our alternatives page lists the published prices.

Where can a restaurant owner report a problem?

The FTC's release points people to ReportFraud.ftc.gov and its Consumer Response Center at 877-382-4357.
