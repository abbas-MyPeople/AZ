How to add AI ordering without losing guests

Keep a person reachable, start AI on the calls it handles best, and count hang-ups from the first day. In September 2026 Burger King cut its AI drive-thru test to about 50 to 70 locations after guests drove off when AI was the only way to order (Nation's Restaurant News, Sep 24, 2026, checked Oct 2026).

The same month, Taco Bell's parent said its drive-thru AI is raising staff retention by making jobs easier (Fortune, Oct 14, 2026, checked Oct 2026). Both run voice AI. The rollout made the difference.

The steps

Seven steps to add AI ordering and keep your guests

  1. Start where guests do not see it.Forecasting, scheduling and inventory drew the least pushback in Restaurant Dive's reporting. A few weeks there teaches your team how AI tools behave before one talks to a guest. See AI scheduling compared.
  2. Never make AI the only door.Burger King's lesson. During the ramp, a guest who wants a person can reach one. Say that in the greeting, briefly.
  3. Give AI the calls it does well first.Hours, location, "is my order ready," menu questions and simple pickup orders. Keep complaints, large parties and odd requests with staff until the AI has proven itself on the easy ones.
  4. Run AI and staff side by side first.Route some calls to the AI and the rest to staff, then shift the share as results come in. After about three months the AI carries nearly all calls. A month-one hiccup is on schedule. Our 90-day rollout plan breaks this into two-week blocks.
  5. Count the right things from day one.Hang-ups during the greeting, calls passed to a person, orders that got paid, and callers who call back within a week. Presto's 10% staff step-in rate is a useful yardstick for your own.
  6. Listen to calls every week.Each wrong answer becomes a fix. Misheard names and dish names are the most common early misses, and every one you correct stays corrected.
  7. Tell staff first, and tell them what gets easier.Taco Bell's retention point only holds if staff see fewer interruptions and fewer remakes. Show them the call log in week one.

Questions owners ask

Do customers like ordering from AI?

Many do not, yet. A Technomic survey shared at FS/TEC in September 2026 found 23% of people find an AI drive-thru bot appealing and 46% find it unappealing, while 46% like ordering from an employee best. Guests accept AI more when it is fast, accurate and a person is still reachable.

Why did Burger King scale back its AI drive-thru?

Its tech lead said that forcing AI as the only way to order did not go well and that guests in pilot restaurants drove off because they did not want to talk to a bot. In September 2026 it was testing in about 50 to 70 locations and building an easier way to reach a person.

How long does an AI phone host take to work well?

Plan on about three months of hybrid running, where the AI takes some calls and staff take the rest while every call is reviewed and the answers improved. After that it can carry nearly all calls.

What should AI handle first in a restaurant?

Behind-the-scenes jobs like forecasting and scheduling, then simple guest questions such as hours, location and order status, then pickup orders. Leave complaints and large or unusual orders with staff until the AI has proven itself.

How do I know if AI ordering is working?

Track hang-ups during the greeting, calls passed to a person, orders that got paid, and repeat callers within a week. Compare each week with the one before. Presto reports workers stepping in on about 10% of its drive-thru orders, a useful yardstick.
