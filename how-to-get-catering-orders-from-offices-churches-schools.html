How to get catering orders from local offices, churches and schools

List every organisation within your delivery radius that feeds groups, find the one person who places the order, send them a short email with tray sizes and how many each feeds, and make ordering and paying a single link. Then follow their calendar: meetings, holidays, school events and religious seasons bring repeat orders. Business email is still covered by the CAN-SPAM Act, so include your address and honour opt-outs.

Step by step

How to build and work the list

Draw your radius

Use the distance you can deliver a hot tray on time. Everything outside it is noise.

List the organisations

Search Google Maps inside that radius for offices, faith groups, schools, clinics and clubs. Write down each one's public website and general email.

Find the person who orders

Office managers, event coordinators and PTA officers. A short call to the front desk asking who handles lunches is often fastest.

Send one short email

Two sentences on what you cook, tray sizes and how many each feeds, one photo, one link to order. Include your address and an opt-out.

Make ordering one link

Trays, headcount guide, date and time, payment up front. A buyer who can order at 9 PM without calling will.

Answer catering calls every time

Many buyers still call. An AI phone host can take the date, headcount and dishes and pass you a summary to close.

Follow their calendar

Note each buyer's events: holidays, Ramadan, the school year, quarter-end meetings. Write before the date, not after.

Stay legal

The email rules that apply to business email

The FTC is plain about it: CAN-SPAM "makes no exception for business-to-business email". For catering outreach that means:

  • A subject line that matches what is inside.
  • Your valid physical postal address in every email.
  • A clear way to opt out, honoured within 10 business days.

Text messages have stricter consent rules than email. Use email for first contact and text only people who asked you to.

The first email

What to put in the first catering email

Buyers skim. The email that gets an order answers their three questions in under a minute: what will it cost per person, will it feed everyone, and how do I order without a phone call.

  • A headcount guide. Say how many people each tray feeds. Ours says a half tray feeds 8 to 10 and a full tray 18 to 20.
  • Lead time. How far ahead they need to order, and the latest time you accept same-day orders if you do.
  • Dietary notes. Vegetarian, halal, nut-free, mild options, written plainly.
  • Delivery area and timing. Where you deliver and how early food arrives before the event.
  • One link and one phone number. No attachments, no PDF menus.

Questions owners ask

How do I get corporate catering clients for my restaurant?

Find the office managers within your delivery radius, send a short email with tray sizes and a one-link order page, and deliver on time. Repeat orders come from reliability.

Can I cold email businesses about catering?

Yes, within CAN-SPAM: an honest subject line, your postal address, and an opt-out you honour within 10 business days. The law applies to business email too.

How do churches and mosques order catering?

Usually through an events coordinator or a volunteer committee, for weekly gatherings, holidays and community events. Clear per-person cost and large trays matter most.

Do I need ezCater or a catering marketplace?

Marketplaces bring buyers but charge a commission on each order. Your own list and ordering link cost less per order but take work to build. Many restaurants use both.
