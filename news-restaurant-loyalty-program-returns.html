Restaurant loyalty programs pay back 9%: what it means for independent restaurants

The Points Guy's "State of Loyalty 2026" report, covered on October 23, 2026, found that restaurant rewards programs give back a median of 9% of what a guest spends, about three times retail's median of roughly 3% (Nation's Restaurant News, Oct 23, 2026, checked Oct 2026).

Guests want free food most, and only half of rewards members say a program changes where they eat. For an independent restaurant, a simple reward that arrives early does more than a points scheme, and taste and accuracy still decide the visit.

What guests said

Four findings that matter more than the ranking

Free food wins

84% of guests named free food as the top benefit. 74% chose a dollar discount on the whole bill.

Restaurants get used

Restaurant programs had 47% of guests enrolled and 22% redeeming regularly, ahead of fuel, retail, airline and hotel programs.

Loyalty is not the reason

Only 50% of dining rewards members said a program affected where they eat.

Food still decides

A Technomic study in the same article found 90% call taste and quality important or very important, ahead of portion, accuracy, service, cleanliness and value. Loyalty ranked below all of them.

All figures from NRN, Oct 23, 2026, checked Oct 2026.

So what for one restaurant

What an independent should copy, and what to skip

Chain habitCopy or skipWhy
A first reward that arrives fast (Domino's)CopyA guest who earns something on the second visit has a reason for a third. A reward 10 visits away is easy to forget.
The reward is a food itemCopyFree food is what guests value most, and a drink or side costs you far less than its menu price.
Points tables and tiersSkipTiers suit chains with millions of members. A neighbourhood restaurant needs one rule a guest can remember.
A separate app to downloadSkipMost guests will not install an app for one restaurant. A phone number at checkout is enough to know who came back.
Discounts for everyone, all the timeSkipYour regulars already come. Spend the reward on guests who have not been back in a while.

Set it up

A simple reward in five steps

  1. Pick one reward with a real cost under a few dollars.A drink, a dessert, a side. Work out what it costs you, not what it sells for.
  2. Make it reachable in two or three visits.A fast first reward does more than a big one far away.
  3. Use the phone number as the card.Ask for it at checkout, on the ordering page and on the phone. No app, no plastic.
  4. Text the guest when the reward is ready.A reward the guest forgets about brings nobody back.
  5. Check who came back after 30 days.Count repeat guests before and after. If the number does not move, change the reward.

Questions owners ask

Which restaurant loyalty program gives the best return?

In The Points Guy's State of Loyalty 2026, Domino's Rewards had the best first-reward path: spend $11.56 and get a free 20-oz soda worth $2.89, a 25% return. MyMcDonald's was next at 16.6%.

How much do restaurant loyalty programs give back?

The median across 10 of the largest dining programs is 9% of spend, according to The Points Guy, about three times the retail median of roughly 3%.

Which restaurant loyalty program gives the least back?

Subway Sub Club, at 5%: members spend $40 to get $2 back. Chipotle was 5.6% and Starbucks 6%.

Do loyalty programs make guests come back to restaurants?

Only partly. Half of dining rewards members said a program affected where they eat. A Technomic study found taste and quality, accuracy, service and value matter more than loyalty programs.

What loyalty program should a small restaurant use?

One simple reward, usually a food item, reachable in two or three visits, tracked by the guest's phone number with no app to download, and a text when the reward is ready.
