Uber Eats and Owner offer free AI-built websites: what it means for independent restaurants

At its first product event for businesses, Delivered, on November 16, 2026, Uber Eats said restaurants on its platform can create a free AI-generated website through Owner.com, with commission-free online ordering, by uploading their menu, photos and reviews (Nation's Restaurant News, Nov 17, 2026, checked Nov 2026). Owner's other tools, such as a branded app and marketing, are offered at 60% off.

A free website with your own ordering is worth taking if you have none. Before you switch an existing site, ask who pays the guest fee, how long free lasts and who owns the guest list.

Why it matters

What a delivery app gains by building your website

  • Your own ordering, at no cost to start. Many independents still send guests to a delivery app because they have no ordering page of their own. A free site with commission-free ordering closes that gap.
  • Uber stays in the loop. The site comes through the app you already pay commission to. Ask whether delivery from the site runs on Uber's drivers, and at what per-trip price. Compare that cost in Uber Direct vs DoorDash Drive.
  • Websites are becoming free. Clover added an AI Website Builder for its U.S. merchants on November 10, 2026, aimed at online stores (PYMNTS, Nov 10, 2026, checked Nov 2026). The site itself is no longer the hard part. Ranking, the ordering experience and the guest list are.

This part is our reading. Neither Uber nor Owner explained how orders from the free site are delivered or billed.

What to do

Five questions before you say yes

  1. Does my guest pay a fee on the free site?On Owner's paid plans, guests pay a 5% order support fee. Ask if the same applies. A fee at checkout pushes some guests back to the app.
  2. How long is it free, and what does it cost after?Get the end date and the price after it in writing.
  3. Who delivers, and at what price per trip?If delivery runs on Uber couriers, ask the per-trip charge and how much of it the guest sees.
  4. Do I get the guest's name, phone and email, and can I export them?The guest list is the reason to have your own ordering at all. See building a guest list without delivery apps.
  5. What happens to my domain if I leave?Keep the domain registered in your name, so the site can move without losing what it has earned on Google.

If you already have a website that ranks, keep it and add a commission-free order button. Compare tools in commission-free ordering systems.

Questions owners ask

Is the Uber Eats Owner website really free?

Nation's Restaurant News reported the website service as free for Uber Eats restaurants, with Owner's app and marketing at 60% off. No end date or conditions were reported, so get them in writing.

Does the Owner website include online ordering?

Yes. The site includes commission-free online ordering, according to the November 17, 2026 report.

Do guests pay a fee when ordering from an Owner website?

On Owner's paid plans, guests pay a 5% order support fee. Whether the free Uber Eats version carries the same fee was not reported.

What else did Uber Eats announce at Delivered?

A Merchant Assistant chatbot for Uber Eats results, a Local Eats section in the app, Ride and Dine deals for riders starting in San Francisco in January, a Reward Center pilot with ad credits, and multi-location grouping tools. Uber said all are free.

Should I replace my current restaurant website with the free one?

Only if your current site does not rank or take orders. If it does, keep it and add your own commission-free ordering. Moving a site can cost you search traffic.

How much does Owner.com cost normally?

Owner lists Flexible at $249 a month plus a 5% restaurant fee per order, and Flat Rate at $499 a month with no restaurant fee. Guests pay a 5% order support fee on both.
