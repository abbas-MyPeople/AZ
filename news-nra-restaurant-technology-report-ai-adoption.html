Only 3% of restaurants run AI companywide: what the NRA technology report means for independent owners

The National Restaurant Association's November 2026 technology report found that about 39% of operators use AI in some form, but only about 3% use it across the whole business (Nation's Restaurant News, Nov 10, 2026, checked Nov 2026). The survey covered 212 operators between June 18 and July 18 (MarketScale, Nov 11, 2026, checked Nov 2026).

Complexity, not price, is the top worry. For an independent owner, that means the hard part of AI is fitting it into the systems you already run and training your team on it, more than picking the tool.

Attitudes split three ways. NRN reports 42% of operators call themselves cautious about AI, 26% excited and 18% skeptical. Among operators already using AI, the most common use is personalized marketing and outreach (58%), then analyzing guest feedback (48%). Data security and privacy is a concern for 35%.

Guests

What guests expect from restaurant technology

The same report covers the guest side. MarketScale's write-up says technology options influence where guests choose to eat for 77% of delivery customers, 66% of takeout customers and 65% of drive-thru customers. About 7 in 10 consumers would order and pay through an app, website or kiosk if one were offered. And 65% of operators say technology improves the hospitality they provide (MarketScale, Nov 11, 2026, checked Nov 2026).

Put next to the 3% figure, that is the gap. Guests already pick restaurants partly on how easy it is to order and pay. Most operators have not connected AI to the rest of their business yet.

Where owners have moved

  • Online ordering and pay on the guest's phone
  • AI for marketing messages and reading reviews
  • Small tests of one AI tool at a time

Where most have not

  • AI tied into the POS, phone, ordering and guest list together
  • Staff trained on what the AI does and when to step in
  • A clear measure of what the tool returned
Read with care

What the survey can and cannot tell you

  • It is small. 212 operators, and MarketScale calls the percentages directional, good for planning but not exact market sizing.
  • It was taken in summer. The answers date from June 18 to July 18, 2026, before the fall run of AI launches from Toast, Square and DoorDash.
  • Read the sponsors. DoorDash was the presenting sponsor and PayPal a supporting sponsor (MarketScale, Nov 11, 2026, checked Nov 2026). That does not make the numbers wrong. It is worth knowing when the report says to connect your ordering and payments.
  • Earlier figure. NRN notes adoption looks higher than the association's State of the Industry report from early 2026, which put AI tool use at 26%.
What to do

Six steps if you are one of the 97%

  1. List what you already run.POS, online ordering, phone, reservations, reviews, guest texts. Most of the value in AI comes from connecting these, so start with the map. Our best restaurant POS guide and POS cost guide help if the POS itself needs replacing.
  2. Pick one job, not one tool."Answer every call during the dinner rush" or "text guests who have not been back in 60 days" is a job. Choose the tool after.
  3. Plan the training before you buy.Training was the top request in the survey. Our guide to introducing AI to restaurant staff covers what to tell the team and who steps in when the AI cannot answer.
  4. Run it hybrid first.Let the AI take part of the work while staff cover the rest, then widen it as it proves itself. The 90-day AI rollout plan lays out the order.
  5. Measure one number.Calls answered, orders placed by guests, reviews per week. If you cannot name the number, you cannot tell if it worked.
  6. Read the contract for the exit.Complexity includes getting out. Check notice periods and fees before you sign, as covered in how to cancel a POS contract.
