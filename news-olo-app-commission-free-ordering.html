The Olo App: what it means for independent restaurants

On October 20, 2026, Olo launched the Olo App, a free app where guests order pickup or delivery from many restaurant brands. Restaurants pay no commission. Guests pay a 5% network fee to Olo plus any delivery fee (Nation's Restaurant News, Oct 20, 2026, checked Oct 2026).

For now it is open only to restaurants that already use Olo, which is mostly chains such as Five Guys and Waffle House. An independent restaurant cannot join today, but the launch confirms the model independents can run on their own: list your real menu prices, show any guest fee up front, and keep the guest's details.

What Olo announced

The Olo App in plain words

Olo is the online ordering software behind many chain restaurant websites and apps. Until now guests never saw its name. The new app puts those restaurants in one place, the way DoorDash or Uber Eats does, but with a different fee split.

Who pays what

The fee split, next to the delivery apps

The big change is who carries the cost. On the delivery apps the restaurant pays a commission and often raises menu prices to cover it. Olo's CEO Noah Glass said exactly that: "restaurants have offset that by charging a higher price on the marketplaces" (Restaurant Dive, Oct 21). On the Olo App the guest pays a small, visible fee and the menu price stays the same.

So what for one restaurant

Four things an independent owner can take from this

Guests accept a fee they can see

Olo is betting guests will pay 5% when the menu price is honest and the fee is shown up front. You can run the same test on your own site: same prices as the counter, any service fee printed before checkout.

Price parity is now a selling point

Olo's whole pitch is "same price as in store." If your delivery-app menu is marked up, say plainly on your own site that ordering direct costs less.

The guest list is the prize

Olo sells "guest data flows to you." That is the part the delivery apps keep. Every direct order should leave you with a name and a phone number you can text later. Our guest list guide shows how.

Do not wait for the app

Olo has given no date for opening to restaurants outside its customer base. A small restaurant gets the same benefits today from its own ordering page and a link on Google.

Olo's CEO also said the app is unlikely to shift industry economics right away and may need refinement (Restaurant Dive). Treat it as a signal of where ordering is heading. Your own channel still has to do the work.

What to do this month

Five steps that copy the Olo model on your own channel

  1. Match your counter prices online.Your own ordering page should never cost more than walking in. If you mark up the delivery apps, keep doing that, and say so.
  2. Show every fee before the guest taps pay.A fee the guest sees at the top of the menu feels fair. A fee that appears at the last step feels like a trick.
  3. Put your own order link first on Google.Delivery apps can appear on your Google profile automatically. Our Google Maps guide shows where to set your own link.
  4. Ask for a phone number on every order.That is how you invite the guest back without paying a commission next time.
  5. Count direct orders every week.If the share of direct orders does not grow, something in steps 1 to 4 is broken.

Questions owners ask

What is the Olo App?

The Olo App is a free app, launched October 20, 2026, where guests order pickup or delivery from more than 250 restaurant brands that already use Olo's ordering software, such as Five Guys and Waffle House.

Does the Olo App charge restaurants a commission?

No. Olo says restaurants pay no commission on orders placed through the Olo App. Guests pay a 5% network fee to Olo, plus a delivery fee on delivery orders.

Can an independent restaurant join the Olo App?

Not yet. Olo says the app is initially available to existing Olo customers and will open to all restaurants eventually. It has not given a date. Restaurants not on Olo are asked to request a demo.

Is the Olo App cheaper for guests than DoorDash?

Olo says menu prices in its app match in-store prices, and guests pay a 5% network fee plus delivery. On delivery apps, restaurants often raise menu prices to cover commissions of 15% to 30%, so the total can be higher there. It depends on each restaurant's prices and fees.

Who delivers Olo App orders?

Olo's Dispatch tool requests quotes from delivery services near the restaurant and picks one. Drivers are often from DoorDash, Uber Eats or other services, and some restaurants use their own drivers.

What should an independent restaurant do about the Olo App?

Copy the model on your own channel: match counter prices online, show any fee up front, ask for a phone number on every order and put your own order link first on Google.
