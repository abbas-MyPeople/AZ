The short version

Three tools, three different jobs

Most "kiosk or QR or AI phone" questions come from one worry: too much work for too few people. The three tools remove different work, so the right pick starts with where your guests order today.

Pick by your room

Which one to start with

You run a counter or a food truck line

A kiosk is the only one of the three that replaces a cashier at the counter. Start with one device, count how many guests use voice and how many tap, and decide on a second only after a month of data. The phone host is a good second step if the phone rings while you are at the register.

You run a sit-down room

QR order and pay at the table. No hardware, no cashier, and payment arrives before the kitchen cooks. Our QR pros and cons guide has the trade-offs and how to keep a server for the guests who want one.

You miss calls at rush or after close

An AI phone host answers every call, takes the order, and texts a pay link. Our call-by-call guide shows what it handles and what still needs a person.

You do catering or large orders

Phone and web forms beat a kiosk. Calls about big orders should reach a person or a quote flow, as in winning more catering orders.

What each tool has actually shown

Evidence, not marketing

  • Kiosk: Square has published no accuracy or adoption numbers for the voice layer yet. The one operator quoted in the launch coverage, Boba Nation Grand, says it tested the kiosks "successfully" and plans to expand them this year (Fast Casual, Oct 14, 2026).
  • Voice ordering in general: Presto, which sells drive-thru voice AI, reports about 95% accuracy with a worker stepping in on about 10% of orders (Restaurant Dive, Sep 21, 2026). Those are the vendor's numbers, but they are a fair yardstick for any voice tool: ask a seller for the step-in rate.
  • QR table ordering: At our proof restaurant in Houston, 54% of QR dine-in tickets carried a tip over the 60 days to Oct 15, 2026. Tipping is the real trade-off of pay-first ordering, and we say so openly in our dine-and-dash guide.
  • AI phone host: Our proof restaurant's AI host answered 273 real calls in three weeks, and 40 became paid orders. 1 in 6 calls arrived while the restaurant was closed. A new restaurant runs the host beside staff for about three months, then it handles calls nearly hands-free.

A sequence that works for most independents

  1. Count where orders come from today.Counter, tables, phone, web, apps. Pull one week from your POS.
  2. Fix the biggest leak first.Missed calls, slow table turns or a long counter line. One tool, one problem.
  3. Run it beside your staff.Give each new tool a trial period with a person ready to step in. Count the step-ins.
  4. Add the second tool only after four weeks of data.The numbers tell you whether guests chose the new path.
  5. Keep one guest list.Whichever channel takes the order, capture a phone number or email in your own list. See building a guest list.

Questions owners ask

Should a restaurant buy a Square Kiosk or use QR table ordering?

It depends on where guests order. A kiosk suits a counter line where guests stand and pay. QR ordering suits a dining room where guests sit, and it needs no hardware. Many restaurants only need one of them.

How much does a Square Kiosk cost compared with QR ordering?

Square Kiosk costs $1,999 including a countertop stand plus $99 a month per device, and needs a Square Plus or Premium plan. QR ordering needs no hardware, and its monthly cost depends on the tool you choose.

Can an AI phone host replace a kiosk?

No. They cover different channels. A kiosk takes orders from guests standing at a counter, and a phone host takes orders from callers. A restaurant with a counter line and busy phones may want both.

Which is best for a small full-service restaurant?

QR order and pay at the table plus an AI phone host usually covers a small full-service room. A counter kiosk adds little when guests sit down.

How long does an AI phone host take to set up?

Plan on about three months. For our clients the phone host runs beside staff for the first three months, with only some calls routed to it, and then it handles calls nearly hands-free.
