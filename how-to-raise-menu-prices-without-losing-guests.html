How to raise menu prices without losing guests

Raise prices item by item from your own cost and sales numbers, change every channel on the same day, and watch item counts for four weeks. Across-the-board increases are the ones guests notice.

The timing is tight: in the year to September 2026, full-service menu prices rose 3.8% and limited-service prices 3.2%, while hourly wages rose 3%, so guests' spending power fell (Nation's Restaurant News, Oct 14, 2026, checked Oct 2026). AI pricing tools so far suggest prices and leave the decision to you, and surge-style pricing has drawn backlash every time a chain tried it.

So what: restaurant prices are rising faster than groceries and faster than pay. Every increase makes cooking at home look better. That does not mean you cannot raise prices. It means you choose which ones.

Step by step

Eight steps to raise prices the careful way

  1. Know the cost of every plate.Cost out each dish from current invoices. Food cost as a share of price is the first number; our glossary explains food cost percentage and prime cost.
  2. Sort the menu into four groups.Popular and profitable, popular and thin, profitable but slow, neither. Pull 90 days of item sales from your POS to do it.
  3. Raise the popular, thin-margin items in small steps.These carry the most volume, so a small increase adds the most money. Keep each step small enough that a regular does not stop to look.
  4. Price add-ons and sides first.Extra protein, sauces, bread, drinks. Guests watch the main price and rarely the extras.
  5. Fix or drop the items that are neither.A dish that sells little and earns little takes prep time and shelf space. Rework it or take it off.
  6. Give a value option.A combo or set meal at a clear price keeps price-sensitive guests coming while the rest of the menu goes up.
  7. Change every channel on the same day.Register, website, QR menu, phone script, printed menus and each delivery app. A guest who sees two prices for one dish trusts neither. Delivery-app prices are a separate decision; see whether restaurants lose money on DoorDash.
  8. Watch for four weeks.Item counts for each raised dish, average check (AOV) and guest count. If a dish drops sharply, roll it back or pair it with a value option.
AI pricing tools

What AI menu pricing really does in 2026

  • Toast's Menu Agent rewrites weak menu descriptions and recommends prices. In October 2026 it was in early access (NRN, Oct 7, 2026, checked Oct 2026). It recommends; you decide.
  • McDonald's disputed reports that it uses AI to set prices. It says its tools give franchisees restaurant-specific recommendations and do not "change prices in real-time or at different hours during the day" (Restaurant Dive, Oct 5, 2026, checked Oct 2026).
  • Wendy's backed off a dynamic pricing test in 2024 after "considerable consumer backlash," per the same Restaurant Dive report.

So what: use software to find which items to raise and by how much. Do not let any tool change prices by time of day. Guests read surge pricing as a trick, and the chains that tried it pulled back.

Mistakes to avoid

Four ways price increases go wrong

Every item, same percent

Easy to do, and every guest notices. It also raises your slow items, which does nothing.

Shrinking portions quietly

Guests notice smaller plates faster than higher prices, and they say so in reviews.

Forgetting a channel

An old price on the website or the phone script means a refund, an argument or a one-star review.

No before number

Without last month's item counts, you cannot tell whether the increase cost you guests.

Questions owners ask

How much did restaurant menu prices go up in 2026?

In the year to September 2026, restaurant prices rose 3.5% overall: 3.8% at full-service restaurants and 3.2% at limited-service restaurants, per federal data reported by Nation's Restaurant News on October 14, 2026.

How often should a restaurant raise menu prices?

Review costs every quarter and change prices when a dish's cost has moved, item by item. Small, targeted changes are easier for guests to accept than one large increase across the whole menu.

Should I charge more on DoorDash and Uber Eats?

Many restaurants do, to cover the commission. Whatever you choose, decide it per channel on purpose, and keep prices the same within each channel: your register, website, QR menu and phone should match each other.

Is AI dynamic pricing a good idea for a small restaurant?

Not by time of day. McDonald's says its tools do not change prices during the day, and Wendy's dropped a dynamic pricing test in 2024 after guest backlash. Use AI to suggest which items to raise, and keep the price steady once set.

How do I know if a price increase lost me guests?

Compare item counts, guest count and average check for the four weeks before and after the change. If one raised dish drops sharply, roll it back or add a value option next to it.
